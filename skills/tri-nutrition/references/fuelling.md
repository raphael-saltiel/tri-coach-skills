# Fuelling reference

## Where the figures come from

The ranges come from the joint position statement "Nutrition and Athletic Performance" of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, Dietitians of Canada and the American College of Sports Medicine (2016), read on 12 November 2026:

- under 45 minutes: carbohydrate not needed,
- 45 to 75 minutes of sustained hard effort: small amounts,
- 1 to 2.5 hours: 30 to 60 g per hour,
- over 2.5 to 3 hours: up to 90 g per hour, with products that mix glucose and fructose,
- fluids: a plan that suits most athletes reaches 0.4 to 0.8 litre per hour, to be adjusted to tolerance, heat and sweat.

The statement adds that the athlete should practise to find the plan that suits their gut. These are general figures for healthy adults: a starting range, never a prescription.

## What 20 to 25 g of carbohydrate looks like

Always read the label: products vary.

| Item | Carbohydrate, roughly |
|---|---|
| One standard energy gel | 20 to 25 g |
| 500 ml of sports drink mixed as directed | 30 to 40 g |
| One banana | 20 to 25 g |
| One cereal or fruit bar | 20 to 30 g |
| Two or three soft dates | 25 g |

A gel is taken with water, not with sports drink: together they are too concentrated for many stomachs.

## Steps, an example

An athlete who takes nothing today, with a long ride each week and an olympic-distance goal (target about 40 to 50 g per hour on the bike):

| Long session | Step |
|---|---|
| 1 and 2 | 1 intake at 45 min, with water |
| 3 and 4 | 1 intake at 45 min and 1 at 90 min |
| 5 and 6 | 1 intake every 40 min from 30 min |
| 7 and 8 | 1 intake every 30 min from 30 min: the race rhythm |

One step lasts two long sessions. A step that causes trouble is repeated at the level below. For a 70.3 or an Ironman the same ladder goes on, towards one intake every 20 minutes, over more weeks.

## By race format

| Format | On the bike | On the run |
|---|---|---|
| Sprint | water, perhaps one gel | water at the aid stations |
| Olympic | 30 to 60 g per hour | water, perhaps one gel at half way |
| 70.3 | 60 g per hour, up to 90 once practised | smaller intakes, what the log shows the stomach accepts while running |
| Ironman | same, for much longer: the log matters more than the table | same, and a plan for when nothing appeals any more |

The bike is where fuelling is easiest: the stomach is still. Whatever is not taken on the bike is rarely caught up on the run.

## Before the start

General habits, to be adapted from the athlete's own log: a familiar meal about three hours before the start, nothing new, and water to thirst. The night before is an ordinary dinner the athlete knows well. No "loading" protocol is given here.

## Heat

More fluid, taken more often, and a drink that contains sodium on long efforts. In heat the stomach accepts less: lower the carbohydrate target rather than force it. Signs of real trouble (dizziness, confusion, no longer sweating, chills): stop and seek help.

## What this skill does not do

Daily diet, weight, calories, supplements, and anything tied to a medical condition or a treatment. For those: a doctor or a registered dietitian.
