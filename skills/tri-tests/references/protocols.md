# Test protocols

## Rules for every test

- Rested: no quality session the day before. An easy swim the day before a run test is fine.
- Same conditions each time: same loop, same pool, same trainer, same time of day. Note wind, heat and anything unusual.
- Warm-up and cool-down are part of the test and are written with their duration.
- The effort is the best even effort for the duration. The trap is always the same: starting too fast. The success criterion is always the same: the second half at least as strong as the first.
- Chest pain, faintness or abnormal breathlessness: stop, and seek medical advice.
- A test is done alone. A group changes the effort and the result cannot be compared.

## Bike

**Level 3, 20 minutes on power (home trainer or power meter).**
1. 15 min progressive warm-up, with 3 x 1 min brisk and 1 min easy between.
2. 20 min at the highest power held evenly. On a trainer, switch off ERG mode.
3. 10 min easy cool-down.

Record the average power of the 20 minutes: threshold power is 95 percent of it. With a heart-rate strap, also record the average heart rate of the 20 minutes: it serves as bike threshold heart rate for outdoor rides without a power meter, marked "estimated" and checked against the talk test.

**Level 2, 30 minutes on heart rate.**
1. 15 min progressive warm-up.
2. 30 min at the best even effort, on a steady road without stops or on a trainer. Press lap after 10 minutes.
3. 10 min easy cool-down.

Bike threshold heart rate is the average of the last 20 minutes. Note the distance covered.

**Level 1, 20 minutes on a fixed loop.**
1. 15 min easy warm-up.
2. 20 min at the best even effort from a fixed starting point. Note the distance, or the exact point reached.
3. 10 min easy cool-down.

The benchmark is the distance. Zones stay on perceived effort and the talk test.

## Run

**Levels 2 and 3, 30 minutes.**
1. 15 to 20 min easy warm-up, with 3 strides of 20 seconds.
2. 30 min at the best even effort, alone, on flat ground or a track. Press lap after 10 minutes.
3. 10 to 15 min easy cool-down.

Threshold heart rate is the average of the last 20 minutes. With GPS, threshold pace is the average pace of the 30 minutes.

**Level 1, 30 minutes on a track or a measured loop.**
Same structure. Count the laps and note the distance. The benchmark is the distance.

## Swim, 1000 m (all levels)

In a pool, with a watch or the wall clock.
1. 300 to 400 m easy warm-up, with a few lengths counting strokes.
2. 1000 m continuous, timed. The long start: on the first 200 m, swim long, with ample strokes and fewer strokes per length than usual, at a held-back pace. Then settle into the best even pace.
3. 200 m easy cool-down.

Record: the total time, the time at 500 m if possible, and the strokes per length at the start and near the end. Pace per 100 m is the total time divided by 10.

What to look at on the first 200 m: the pace (not faster than the average of the test) and the stroke count (not higher than at the end). A start with a high stroke count and a fast pace is the classic failed test: the second half collapses. Success: second 500 m at least as fast as the first.

Open-water tests are not used for zones. See the open-water rule before any session outside the pool.

## Endurance check (not a test session)

On long sessions, alone, at the endurance ceiling:
- Level 2 or 3: note the pace on flat ground (run) or the speed or power (bike) at that heart rate. Compared from month to month, it shows the base moving.
- Level 1: time a fixed loop at talk-test effort (full sentences).

Stops longer than two minutes on a solo long session break the drift reading: note them, and do not judge that session.

## Gentle calibration (when a gate is closed)

No maximal effort. The goal is a first, cautious endurance ceiling.
1. 10 min very easy.
2. 3 x 5 min at the highest effort where full sentences are still possible, with 2 min easy between.
3. 5 to 10 min very easy.

Note, on the third block: the heart rate if there is a sensor, and the pace or speed if available. The endurance ceiling is set at that heart rate, marked "estimated". Without a sensor, the ceiling is the talk test itself.

In the pool: 4 x 100 m easy with 30 s rest, noting the time and the strokes per length. It gives a first easy pace, not a threshold.
