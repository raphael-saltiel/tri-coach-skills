# Phases and milestones

## The five phases

Counted back from the race. The taper has a fixed length by format. The other four share the remaining weeks: base about 30 percent, development 25, specific 28, peak 17. The script does the split.

| Phase | Purpose | What it contains | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Build the volume that cannot be caught up later. Technique and habit. | Mostly endurance under the ceiling, swim technique, a little strength, one light brick per block. | The athlete holds the weeks without missing sessions and without pain. |
| Development | Raise the thresholds and bring the weekly volume up to the plateau. | One quality session per sport per week at most, long sessions growing. | The plateau is reached, or the script has warned it will not be. |
| Specific | Train for the race itself. | Bricks every load week, long sessions on terrain like the course, first open-water sessions (never alone) when the race swims outdoors, race-day fuelling practised. | The athlete has done the rehearsal. |
| Peak | Largest weeks, at race rhythm. | Plateau volume, sessions at race effort, last retest. | The last load week is done without digging a hole. |
| Taper | Let freshness rise without losing fitness. | Load cut sharply, short reminders of race effort, nothing new: no new shoes, food, position or session type. | Race day. |

## Plain-words explanations (for the "detailed" level)

Use these wordings, translated, so that the same term is always explained the same way.

| Term | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Ramp | The planned rise of the weekly hours, from what you do today to what the race needs. |
| Load week | A normal training week, a little bigger than the last one. |
| Recovery week | A lighter week, about a third less, every fourth week. The body gets fitter while it recovers, not while it trains: this week is where the three before it pay off. |
| Plateau | The weekly volume the plan climbs to and then holds. |
| Phase | A block of weeks with one purpose. |
| Long session | The longest ride and the longest run of the week. They build the endurance the race is made of. |
| Brick | A run done straight after a ride, to get used to running on cycling legs. |
| Test, retest | A measured effort that sets your zones. It is repeated every eight weeks or so to check that training works. |
| Rehearsal | A practice race at shorter distances, with the kit, the food and the transitions of race day. |
| Taper | The last two or three weeks: much less training, so that you start the race fresh. Fitness does not vanish in two weeks, fatigue does. |
| Milestone | A dated event in the plan: a rehearsal, an intermediate race, the race. |

Why the numbers are what they are:

- **The start**: the plan begins at what you really did last week, because a body adapts from where it is, not from where you wish it were.
- **+10 percent at most**: heart and lungs adapt in weeks, tendons and bones in months. Rising faster is how beginners get injured.
- **Minus 35 percent every fourth week**: enough to recover, not enough to lose fitness.
- **The plateau**: the smaller of what the race format needs and what your life allows. More is not better if it cannot be held.
- **The taper**: the load drops sharply, the rhythm of the week stays.

## Load and recovery weeks

Three load weeks, one recovery week. The recovery week is at 65 percent of the week before it, with no intensity except the test placed at its end. It is a ceiling: less is fine. Never a recovery week right before the taper: the script arranges the blocks so that the taper follows a load week.

## Tests

- Weeks 1 and 2: initial tests, if the gates in the profile are open.
- First recovery week: initial tests that were deferred because a gate was closed.
- Then every recovery week ends with a retest: bike and swim, then run and swim, alternating. Each sport comes back about every eight weeks.
- Skip a retest when that sport was tested less than five weeks before.
- The first retest decides the time goal. Before it, the goal is to finish. A goal revised downwards is not a failure of the preparation.

## Bricks (bike then run)

- From the base phase, one per block, small: a short easy run right after a ride. The point is the habit of running off the bike, not fitness.
- In the specific and peak phases, one every load week.
- Always the same start: first minute walking fast, then five minutes deliberately slow whatever the legs say. The first brick almost always starts too fast.

## Intermediate races

A race before the goal race is a step. It replaces the quality or long session of its week, it is run at the effort the plan gives (often strict endurance for a running race early in the plan), and it never justifies skipping the recovery week. A race with friends or colleagues is run at endurance effort, without a final sprint.

## Rehearsal

On the week marked `rehearsal`: a race of a shorter format if one exists nearby, otherwise a home simulation at reduced distances (swim, then bike, then run, with the race-day kit, food and transitions). For a sprint goal, the simulation is the rehearsal. Nothing in it is new on race day.

## Two sessions a day

Only when single sessions are full: every available day already carries a session of sensible length and the weekly hours still cannot fit. Before that point, a second session adds fatigue and injury risk for nothing. When the athlete asks for doubles early, say no and say why.

## Quality steps

Write the progression of each quality session in advance, by steps: the same total time at effort, in longer blocks. Example for a threshold run: 3x8 min, then 3x10 min, then 2x15 min. One step per block at most. "I could have done more" does not move the athlete one step up: only the same feeling three weeks in a row does.
