# Adjustment rules, with a worked case each

The athlete in these cases is fictional. Planned week: 5 h 20, six sessions.

## A missed session is not made up

Case: Tuesday's quality run is missed because of a late meeting. The athlete suggests doing it on Wednesday before the swim.
Answer: no. Wednesday stays as written. The quality run would then sit the day before Thursday's bike quality, and the week would break the spacing rule. The session is lost, the week ends at 4 h 20, and that is fine once.

## More than 10 percent over the planned hours

Case: by Saturday evening the athlete has done 5 h 10 of a 5 h 20 week, with a 1 h 30 long run still planned on Sunday. The total would reach 6 h 40, 25 percent over.
Answer: stop adding. Sunday becomes 30 easy minutes or rest. The overshoot is noted in the journal. Next week is written from the plan, not from the overshoot.

## Two weeks in a row under 85 percent

Case: 4 h 15 done of 5 h 20 (80 percent), then 4 h 30 of 5 h 50 (77 percent).
Answer: the plan is drifting. Do not write the next, higher week. Ask what drops out (always the same session, the same day, the same sport?). Fix the cause: move the session, shorten it, or lower the hours ceiling. Then tri-plan rebuilds the ramp from the volume really held.

## Pain on two sessions in a row

Case: the shin hurts during Tuesday's run and again during Thursday's short run.
Answer: running is removed for the rest of the week. The swims and the ride stay only if they are pain-free. Nothing is made up. A medical opinion is recommended. No diagnosis is offered: the journal records where, when and at what effort it hurt. If it hurts at rest or in every sport, everything stops.

## "I could have done more"

Case: after 3x8 min at threshold, the athlete says the third block felt easy and asks for 4x8 next week.
Answer: nothing is added. Heart and lungs adapt faster than tendons. The planned step stays 3x8. If the same feeling comes back three weeks in a row, move one step up (3x10), not two.

## Recovery signal

The athlete chose their own three levels at onboarding (a device score, or morning feeling).
- Green: the planned session.
- Orange: the planned session, exactly as written, nothing added.
- Red: 30 easy minutes or rest. The session is not made up the next day.

Case: red on Thursday morning, trainer quality session planned.
Answer: 30 easy minutes of spinning or rest. Friday stays as written. The quality session is lost.

Case: the device has not synced this morning's score yet.
Answer: say so. Do not reuse yesterday's score. Ask how the athlete feels and use that.

## A date or a test moves

Case: the run test planned on Sunday of the recovery week moves to the following Wednesday because of travel.
Answer: reread the fifteen days around it. Tuesday's quality run, which quoted "your new threshold pace", is rewritten with the current value and becomes easy (day before the test). Thursday's bike quality moves to Friday. Every session that mentioned the old test date is rewritten.

## A threshold changes

Case: the retest gives a threshold pace 8 seconds per km faster.
Answer: zones are recomputed (tri-tests), then every coming session with a run pace or heart rate is rewritten with the new numbers. Old numbers left in the calendar are an error.

## Travel or missing equipment

Case: a weekend away without the road bike, long ride of 2 h planned.
Answer: use the fallback written in the session. If there is none: an equivalent on the trainer, a little shorter because there is no freewheeling (1 h 45 in the endurance zone, with cadence changes to pass the time). No trainer either: a long easy run is not an equivalent. Swap days with the long run if the week allows it, otherwise the ride is lost.

## Weather

Case: storm warning on the day of the long ride.
Answer: the fallback is already in the session ("if the road is not rideable: 1 h 45 on the trainer under the endurance ceiling"). The athlete does not have to ask.

## A group outing

Case: a friend who is starting out wants to join Saturday's ride.
Answer: accepted. The session is written as strict endurance. In the review (tri-bilan), only the solo part is judged against the instruction.

## Trainer in ERG mode

Case: on the third block the cadence falls and the resistance locks the legs.
Answer: spin back up before it locks (cadence above 80). If power collapses, finish the block at what holds, without stopping. Next week keeps the same number of blocks instead of adding one.

## A social sport

Case: a game of tennis with a colleague on Friday evening.
Answer: accepted as easy exchanges, about an hour, no match. It counts as easy load in the week. If anything hurts at the warm-up, it is skipped.
