# Race checklist

Adapt it to the race and to the athlete. Everything on it was already used in training or in the rehearsal: nothing new.

## The week before

- Read the athlete guide and the rules on the organiser's page: start time, cut-offs, wetsuit rule, what is compulsory, what the aid stations hand out. Write the date you checked.
- Bike checked: tyres, brakes, gears, bolts. A shop visit happens at least a week before, not the day before.
- The taper sessions as written. No extra session "to be sure".
- Race-day fuelling bought, the products of the tolerance log.
- Travel, parking and bib collection times known.

## The day before

- Collect the bib and the timing chip. Walk the transition area and note the exits.
- A short, easy session if the plan has one.
- Pack by leg, in the order of use (lists below).
- An ordinary dinner the athlete knows well. Water to thirst.
- Tyres inflated in the morning, not in the evening.

## Kit, by leg

| Leg | Items |
|---|---|
| Swim | tri suit or swimsuit, wetsuit if used, two pairs of goggles, the race swim cap, anti-chafing balm for the neck and under the arms, timing chip on the ankle |
| T1 | small towel, helmet, sunglasses, bike shoes, race belt with the bib, socks if used |
| Bike | bike, two bottles, the fuelling of the plan, spare tube, tyre levers, pump or cartridge, bike computer or watch if used |
| T2 | running shoes with elastic laces if practised, cap or visor |
| Run | the fuelling of the plan, anti-chafing protection where the long runs showed it is needed |
| After | warm dry clothes, food, a drink |

## The morning

- The familiar meal, about three hours before the start.
- Arrive early: a rushed set-up costs more than a short night.
- Lay out the transition in the order of use. Check the bike is in an easy gear.
- Check the tyre pressure.
- Wetsuit on with time to spare, anti-chafing balm first.
- A few minutes of easy swimming if the organiser allows it, otherwise arm swings and a short jog.
- Read the pacing plan once more: one line per leg.

## Rain, cold or heat

- Rain: lower tyre pressure a little as practised, brake earlier, anti-chafing protection everywhere fabric rubs.
- Cold: a layer for the bike in T1, gloves if practised.
- Heat: more fluid, more often, and a lower effort from the start. The cut-off margin tells how much can be given away.

## After the line

- Keep walking a few minutes, drink, eat, get warm.
- Note the times of each leg and each transition, and how the fuelling went: the next estimate will use them.
