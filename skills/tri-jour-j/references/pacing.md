# Pacing plan

One instruction per leg, at the athlete's level, written in `athlete/jour-j.md`.

## Swim

- Start to the side or at the back of the group unless the athlete is a confident swimmer.
- The first 200 m long: ample strokes, fewer strokes than feels natural in the excitement, breathing settled. Same start as the 1000 m test.
- Then the test rhythm. Sight every six to ten strokes in open water.
- If breathing runs away: breaststroke or float on the back for a few seconds, then start again. Hold on to a safety boat if needed: it is allowed to rest there.
- Open water was practised before race day, never alone.

## Bike

| Level | Instruction |
|---|---|
| 3 | A power ceiling for the whole leg (the race power). On climbs, hold it and accept the speed. Do not chase the average: coasting downhill is fine. |
| 2 | A heart-rate ceiling. As a starting point, the top of the endurance zone for a 70.3 or an Ironman, the tempo zone for a sprint or an olympic. Heart rate reads high in the first minutes after the swim: ride easy until it settles. |
| 1 | Full sentences on the flat for a 70.3 or an Ironman, short sentences for a sprint or an olympic. On climbs, one notch harder at most. |

- The first ten minutes are easier than the plan: the legs lie after the swim.
- Fuelling follows the timeline, started early.
- The last five minutes spin easily, to prepare the run.

## Run

- First minute walking fast, or at a very easy jog.
- Then five minutes deliberately slow, whatever the legs say. They will say "fast": they are wrong.
- Then the planned effort: the pace from the estimate (level 3), the heart-rate ceiling (level 2), or the talk test (level 1).
- Walk through the aid stations if it helps to drink. Ten seconds of walking cost less than a missed drink.
- If it goes badly: walk one minute, run four, and keep moving. The cut-off margin from the estimate says how much room there is.

## Transitions

- Same order as in the rehearsal, every time.
- T1: wetsuit off, helmet on and fastened before touching the bike, shoes, go. Mount after the line.
- T2: dismount before the line, rack the bike, helmet off, shoes, go.
- Walk through the transition area the day before or in the morning: where the bike is, where the exits are.

## When the plan and the body disagree

Chest pain, faintness or abnormal breathlessness: stop and ask for help from a marshal. No race is worth it.
