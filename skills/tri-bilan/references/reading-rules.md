# Reading a session

## What "physiological reading" means

Two or three sentences on what the numbers say about the body. Pick what applies:

- **Evenness across blocks.** Same pace or power from the first block to the last: the effort was the right one. Each block slower than the one before: the start was too fast.
- **Drift.** On a steady endurance session, heart rate that climbs while pace or power stays the same. Compare the first half with the second. A small drift on a long session is normal. A large one points to heat, dehydration, lack of fuel, or an effort above the ceiling. Do not put a number on "large" that the athlete's own history does not support: compare with their previous long sessions.
- **Heart rate against the ceiling.** Time spent above the endurance ceiling on an endurance session.
- **Effort against result.** A normal pace at a higher effort than usual: fatigue, heat, or a bad day. Say it, and do not turn one session into a trend.
- **Stroke count in the pool.** More strokes per length at the same pace: technique fading with fatigue.
- **Level 1.** The reading rests on the talk test and the effort out of 10 through the session, and on the benchmark distances and times.

One session is one data point. A trend needs at least three.

## Segments

A session with company, a long stop, a puncture or a detour is cut into segments:

1. alone before,
2. accompanied or interrupted,
3. alone after.

Only the segments where the athlete could follow the instruction are judged. The others count in the hours of the week, as easy load, and are not judged. Say which part you judged.

## Stops

On a solo long session, stops longer than two minutes let the heart rate fall and break the drift reading. Count the session in the hours, judge the ceiling, and do not judge the drift.

## Duplicates

A watch, a bike computer, a trainer app and a recovery band can all record the same session. Rule:

- One recording per session: the athlete's main device for that sport, noted in `athlete/connect.md` or in the profile.
- Same sport and same start time, within a few minutes: it is a copy. Ignore it.
- A recovery band's automatic "activity" is always a copy when another device recorded the session.
- Weekly hours are counted on the kept recordings only.

## Easy sessions done too fast

An endurance swim swum at the pace of the repeats, an easy run above the ceiling: the plan counts the hours and not the intensity, so this load is invisible to the ramp. Name it at the review. Twice in the same week, it explains a heavy week better than any other cause.

## Estimated zones

Before the first test, ceilings are deliberately low. A session "above the ceiling" may only show that the estimate is cautious. Say it, do not blame the athlete, and wait for the test.

## The trainer in ERG mode

- Cadence under 80 on a block: the resistance locks the legs. The fix is to spin back up early, not to push harder.
- Power collapsing on the last block: the athlete finishes at what holds, without stopping. At the review: same number of blocks next week, no step up.
- The threshold power set in the trainer app must be the one in `athlete/zones.md`. Blocks that all read a few watts low usually mean the app has an old value.

## Recovery signal

Three levels, with thresholds chosen by the athlete at onboarding (a device score or the morning feeling). Use today's reading. If the device has not synced yet, say so and ask how the athlete feels. Never reuse yesterday's reading.
