# The debrief, worked

Always the same five parts, in the same order. The athlete in these examples is fictional. Write in the athlete's language.

## Level 3, from Intervals.icu data: a threshold run

Planned (week file): 12 min warm-up under the endurance ceiling, 3x6 min at threshold pace 5:15 per km with 2 min jog, 6 min cool-down. Success criterion: third block as fast as the first.

> **Planned.** 3x6 min at 5:15 per km, 2 min jog between, 40 min in all. Success: third block as fast as the first.
>
> **Done.** 41 min, 6.6 km. Blocks at 5:08, 5:14, 5:21 per km. Warm-up under the ceiling. Cool-down done.
>
> **Reading.** You started 7 seconds per km too fast and paid for it: each block is slower than the one before, 13 seconds lost from the first to the third. Heart rate was still climbing at the end of the third block while the pace was dropping. That is the signature of a first block run above threshold.
>
> **Point of attention.** The first block. Lock it at 5:15, even if it feels easy for the first two minutes.
>
> **Next.** Tomorrow: the 5x100 m swim, as written. Nothing changes this week. Next Tuesday stays at 3x6: you move to 3x8 once the three blocks are level.

## Level 1, from a story: the same session without a device

Planned: 12 min easy, 3x6 min at 7 to 8 out of 10 (a few words at a time), 2 min jog, 6 min easy. Success criterion: the third block feels like the first.

The athlete says: "Did it. First block felt great, I was flying. Third one I was hanging on, couldn't say a word."

> **Planned.** 3x6 min at 7 to 8 out of 10, where you can still say a few words. Success: the third block feels like the first.
>
> **Done.** The three blocks, 40 min in all. First block "flying", third block "couldn't say a word".
>
> **Reading.** "Couldn't say a word" is 9 out of 10, above the session. "Flying" on the first block means it was already too fast: at the right effort the first block feels almost too easy. You did a harder session than the one written.
>
> **Point of attention.** Use the talk test on the first block: if you cannot say four or five words in a row, slow down.
>
> **Next.** Tomorrow: the easy swim, as written. Nothing changes. Same session next week, same three blocks.

## Level 2, from a screenshot: a long ride

Planned: 2 h under the endurance ceiling (heart rate), then 10 min run starting with one minute of fast walking.

The screenshot shows: 2 h 04, 46 km, average heart rate 6 beats under the ceiling, maximum 9 beats above it. The time in zones is cut off.

> **Planned.** 2 h under your ceiling, then 10 min of running, first minute walking fast.
>
> **Done.** 2 h 04, 46 km. Average 6 beats under the ceiling. Maximum 9 beats above it. Your screenshot cuts the time in zones: how long were you above the ceiling, roughly?

After the answer ("the two climbs, maybe five minutes in all"):

> **Reading.** Five minutes above the ceiling in two hours, on climbs: the session is an endurance ride. The average well under the ceiling is not a fault, there is no floor. Did the run start with the minute of walking?
>
> **Point of attention.** None.
>
> **Next.** Tomorrow: the long run under the ceiling. Nothing changes.

## The same debrief at the "detailed" level

Same five parts, same order, same verdict as the level 3 example above. What is added is the reason behind each statement, and each new term explained once (then added to `athlete/lexique.md`).

> **Planned.** 3x6 min at 5:15 per km, 2 min jog between, 40 min in all. 5:15 is your threshold pace: the fastest pace you can hold steadily for about an hour, measured by your 30 minute test. Success: third block as fast as the first.
>
> **Done.** 41 min, 6.6 km. Blocks at 5:08, 5:14, 5:21 per km. Warm-up under the ceiling. Cool-down done.
>
> **Reading.** You started 7 seconds per km too fast and paid for it: each block is slower than the one before, 13 seconds lost from the first to the third. Here is why. Above threshold, fatigue builds faster than your body clears it. Seven seconds per km does not feel like much in the first minutes, but the debt is there, and the second and third blocks repay it. Your heart rate was still climbing at the end of the third block while your pace was dropping: the body was working harder to go slower. On blocks this short I judge the pace and not the heart rate, because the heart takes a minute or two to catch up with the effort.
>
> **Point of attention.** The first block. Lock it at 5:15, even if it feels easy for the first two minutes: at the right pace, the first block is supposed to feel almost too easy. Check your watch at 200 m and at 400 m, then stop looking.
>
> **Next.** Tomorrow: the 5x100 m swim, as written. Nothing changes this week: one session run a little too hard is not a reason to touch the plan. Next Tuesday stays at 3x6. You move to 3x8 once the three blocks are level, because holding the pace matters more than adding minutes.

## What a debrief never contains

- Congratulations for doing what was written.
- More than one point of attention.
- A figure the athlete did not give and the data does not show.
- A diagnosis.
- An extra session to make up for a missed one.
