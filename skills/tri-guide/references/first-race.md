# Choosing a first race

## The format

| Format | Minimum weeks of preparation in this pack | For whom, as a first triathlon |
|---|---|---|
| Sprint (S) | 8 | Almost everyone who can swim a few lengths, ride an hour and jog half an hour. The natural first race. |
| Olympic (M) | 12 | Someone already active in at least two of the three sports. |
| 70.3 | 16 | Someone with a real endurance base and a life that allows about ten hours a week at the peak. |
| Ironman | 24 | Not recommended as a first triathlon. Say so plainly, even when the weeks are there, and suggest a shorter race first. |

These minimums are choices of this pack for a beginner. Below them, the goal is risky: aim only to finish, or pick a later or shorter race.

## What makes a race beginner-friendly

- **The swim.** The first fear and the first risk. A pool swim or a calm lake is easier than the sea or a river. Look at how the start works: small waves of swimmers are gentler than a mass start.
- **The water temperature**, and the organiser's rule on wetsuits.
- **The bike course.** Look at the real elevation gain, not the description. A flat or rolling course, on roads closed or protected from traffic.
- **The cut-offs.** Generous ones leave room for a bad patch. Compare them with an honest estimate (tri-jour-j).
- **The start time**, and with a late start, the daylight left at the finish.
- **The date.** Count the weeks from today. A race too close is the most common mistake.
- **The distance from home.** A local race removes travel and a bad night.
- **The atmosphere.** Some races have a beginners' wave or a discovery format. Ask the organiser.

## Questions to ask the athlete

1. How far do you swim without stopping, and in what kind of water have you already swum?
2. What do you do in a normal week today, in each sport?
3. How many hours a week can you honestly give?
4. Which date do you have in mind, and is it fixed?
5. What worries you most?

## Reading the answers

- Cannot yet swim the race distance in a pool: choose a race far enough away to get there, and a pool or lake swim.
- A fixed date that is too close for the format: say it, and offer the shorter format on the same day if the race has one.
- Wants a long format "to have a real goal": a sprint or an olympic first makes the long one safer, and nobody regrets it.

Everything about a given race (distances, course, cut-offs, rules, temperature) is read on the organiser's page, with the date it was read. Once the race is chosen: tri-onboarding.
