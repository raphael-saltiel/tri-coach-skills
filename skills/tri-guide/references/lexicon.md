# Lexicon

Plain-words explanations, to translate into the athlete's language. One wording per term, used everywhere in the pack.

## The sport

| Term | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Triathlon | Swim, then bike, then run, in that order, without stopping the clock. |
| Formats | Sprint or S: 750 m swim, 20 km bike, 5 km run. Olympic or M: 1.5 km, 40 km, 10 km. 70.3 or half: 1.9 km, 90 km, 21.1 km. Ironman or full: 3.8 km, 180 km, 42.2 km. Distances vary a little from race to race. |
| Transition | The change from one sport to the next. T1 is swim to bike, T2 is bike to run. The clock keeps running. |
| Transition area | The fenced zone where bikes are racked and kit is laid out. |
| Brick | A run done straight after a ride, in training, to get used to running on cycling legs. |
| Open water | Swimming in a lake, the sea or a river, as opposed to a pool. |
| Wetsuit | A neoprene suit for cold open water. It keeps you warm and helps you float. |
| Sighting | Lifting the eyes forward while swimming to check the direction. |
| Drafting | Riding or swimming close behind someone to save effort. Allowed in the swim, forbidden on the bike in most amateur races. |
| Cut-off | A time limit at a point of the race. An athlete who arrives after it is stopped. |
| Aid station | A table on the course with water and sometimes food. |
| Bib | The race number, worn on a belt. |
| Timing chip | A small tag, usually on the ankle, that records your times. |

## Training

| Term | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Threshold | The hardest effort you can hold steadily for about an hour. Everything else is set from it. |
| Zone | A band of effort with one purpose. |
| Endurance | Comfortable effort at which you can hold a conversation. Most of the training. |
| Endurance ceiling | The top of the endurance zone. Stay under it. There is no minimum. |
| Quality session | A session with blocks at a sustained effort. One per sport per week at most. |
| Strides | Short, relaxed accelerations of about 20 seconds, never a sprint. |
| RPE, effort out of 10 | Your own rating of how hard it feels, from 1 (strolling) to 10 (all out). |
| Talk test | How much you can say while moving. Full sentences: endurance. A few words: threshold. |
| Warm-up, cool-down | The easy minutes before and after the main part. They are part of the session. |
| Long session | The longest ride and the longest run of the week. |
| Load week | A normal training week, a little bigger than the last one. |
| Recovery week | A lighter week, every fourth week, where the body absorbs the three before it. |
| Ramp | The planned rise of the weekly hours. |
| Taper | The last two or three weeks before the race: much less training, to start fresh. |
| Test, benchmark | A measured effort repeated every eight weeks or so, to set zones and check progress. |
| Drift | Heart rate creeping up while pace or power stays the same. |
| Fallback | The replacement session written in advance for the day the planned one is impossible. |

## Measures

| Term | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Heart rate | Beats per minute. It follows the effort with a delay of a minute or two. |
| Power | The effort put into the pedals, in watts. It reacts at once and ignores wind and slope. |
| Threshold power, FTP | Your threshold, read in watts. |
| Pace | Time per kilometre on the run, per 100 m in the pool. |
| Cadence | Pedal turns per minute on the bike, steps per minute on the run. |
| Strokes per length | Arm strokes needed to cross the pool. Fewer at the same speed means a more efficient stroke. |
| Home trainer | A stand that turns a bike into an indoor bike. Some measure power. |
| ERG mode | A trainer setting that imposes the watts whatever the cadence. |
| Power meter | A sensor on the bike that measures power outdoors. |
| Elevation gain | The total metres climbed on a course. |

## The bike

| Term | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Hoods | The top of the brake levers, the default hand position on a road bike. |
| Drops | The lower, curved part of the handlebar. Lower position, less air to push. |
| Aero bars | Extensions that let you rest on the forearms. The most aerodynamic position, if it can be held. |
| Clipless pedals | Pedals that lock the shoe in. Not needed for a first race. |
| Race belt | An elastic belt that holds the bib, turned to the back on the bike and to the front on the run. |
