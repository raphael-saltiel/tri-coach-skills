# Race day and the usual rules

Rules differ between countries, federations and races, and they change. What follows are the usual principles, to explain why they exist. The organiser's athlete guide and the federation's current rules are the reference: read them the week before, and write down the date.

## The day, in order

1. **Bib collection.** With an identity document, and whatever the organiser requires (licence, medical certificate or equivalent, depending on the country).
2. **Setting up the transition.** Rack the bike at your number, lay out the kit in the order of use, walk the entries and exits.
3. **The briefing.** Listen to it: it gives the day's changes.
4. **The swim.** Start in your wave, follow the buoys.
5. **T1.** Wetsuit off, helmet on and fastened, then the bike.
6. **The bike.**
7. **T2.** Bike racked, then helmet off, shoes on.
8. **The run**, then the finish line.

## Rules found almost everywhere, and why

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Helmet on and fastened before touching the bike, until the bike is racked again | Safety. It is the rule beginners break most often, and it is penalised. |
| Mount the bike after the mount line, dismount before the dismount line | No riding inside the transition area, where people are on foot. |
| No drafting on the bike in most amateur races: keep a gap behind the rider in front and pass within a set time | Fairness and safety. The size of the gap depends on the race: read it in the guide. |
| Ride on the side of the road the organiser says, pass on the other | So that faster riders can pass safely. |
| The bib visible: at the back on the bike, at the front on the run | So that marshals can identify you. |
| No outside help, no pacer, no one running or riding beside you | The race is individual. |
| No headphones | You must hear marshals and traffic. |
| No litter outside the zones provided | Penalised, and it closes courses for future races. |
| Obey the marshals and the traffic rules where roads are open | Safety. |
| Cut-offs are applied | An athlete past a cut-off is stopped, for their safety and the volunteers'. |

## The swim

- The wetsuit may be compulsory, allowed or forbidden depending on the water temperature. The thresholds depend on the federation and the distance: read the guide, do not rely on a figure heard elsewhere.
- If things go wrong: turn on your back, breathe, raise an arm to call a safety boat. Holding on to a boat or a buoy to rest is generally allowed as long as it does not move you forward.
- Race day is not the first time in open water. The first sessions are done before, never alone.

## Penalties

Depending on the rule and the race: a warning, a time penalty served in a penalty area, or disqualification. When a marshal shows a card, stop and ask what to do.

## What a beginner should really remember

Helmet fastened whenever the bike is in your hands. No drafting on the bike. Bib visible. Nothing new on race day. And read the athlete guide.
